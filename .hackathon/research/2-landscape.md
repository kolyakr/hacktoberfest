# Existing solutions: what products already do "AI that acts" in customer support, and where do they fall short?

**One-line answer:** The big vendors (Fin/Intercom, Zendesk, Agentforce, Sierra, Decagon, Ada, Gorgias, Freshdesk, Kustomer) already ship AI agents that call APIs, approval steps with a human in the loop, simulations, and QA that feeds back into the bot. The parts they leave open are different. Each one is a closed, expensive platform that marks its own homework: the vendor counts the "resolutions" it bills for. Their QA scores tone and empathy, not whether an action followed policy. Their reasoning logs live inside the vendor's own UI. Published benchmarks show LLM agents still break policy rules and give different results on repeated runs. A one-day open project can't win on features. It can win on **auditability**: actions grounded in a cited policy clause, a visible approval gate, and an independent QA check that tests each action against policy and feeds corrections back into the policy.

## Key findings

### Who acts, and how
- **Fin (formerly Intercom).** Fin acts through *data connectors*, which are HTTP calls that read or write external systems ("changing an order status or user details"). *Procedures* chain these into multi-step flows such as refunds and account updates. Fin keeps no retry: "Fin Procedures do not automatically handle data connector failures," and the docs warn that "a failed lookup must never be treated as proof that an account, charge, or entitlement does not exist." Testing a POST, PUT or DELETE runs it against real data ("testing a DELETE call on a user will actually delete them"). — **high** (https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/9916507-fin-tasks-and-data-connectors-faqs)
- **Fin already has approvals with a human in the loop.** A "Loop in teammate" step pauses the Procedure and shows a "Fin requires your decision" card in the Inbox or in Slack, together with the connector data. The teammate's answer is a True/False or Text field that later steps branch on. If nobody answers before the timeout, the conversation is escalated. There is no dedicated reject button and only those two field types. — **high** (https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/14468561)
- **Fin pricing.** $0.99 per "outcome". An outcome is counted when the customer confirms, when the customer does not ask for more help, or when Fin completes a Procedure, handoffs included. Using Fin on top of another helpdesk has a monthly minimum (the example given is 50 outcomes). Seats cost $19 (new customers), $85 or $132. — **high** (https://www.intercom.com/pricing)
- **Fin Insights.** Gives a "CX Score" for every conversation without a survey, groups conversations into Topics, and shows AI "Suggestions" that "improve Fin's answers in a single click". It was in open beta from May 2025. In other words, Fin already closes the loop from analysis back to the bot, but only for *knowledge content* and only inside Intercom. — **medium** (https://fin.ai/updates/fin-insights)
- **Market consolidation.** Salesforce agreed on 15 June 2026 to buy Fin for about $3.6B. Fin has more than 30,000 customers, and Salesforce cites example deployments "resolving on average 76% of support volume end-to-end". — **high** (https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2026/Salesforce-Signs-Definitive-Agreement-to-Acquire-Fin/)
- **Zendesk Copilot auto assist.** It suggests up to four actions or action flows, and the agent clicks approve. Admins can mark custom actions as *pre-approved* so they run unattended. Zendesk's own risk notes say pre-approved actions "may run in a different order than the procedure specifies" and "might run before those conditions are satisfied". It recommends keeping write actions such as refunds agent-approved. Other limits: "Actions don't work on AI agent tickets", auto assist can't see unsaved field edits, it needs the Copilot add-on and the Growth plan or higher. — **high** (https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/9174548349978-About-actions-for-auto-assist-and-action-flows)
- **Zendesk pricing for AI agents.** Outcome-based since August 2024: "customers only pay for issues resolved autonomously by AI". A starter allowance comes with every Suite and Support plan. The public page does not define the resolution unit. — **high** (https://www.zendesk.com/newsroom/articles/zendesk-outcome-based-pricing/)
- **Salesforce Agentforce.** $2 per conversation, or Flex Credits at $500 per 100k credits (20 credits = one action, about $0.10). "Help Agent Resolutions" are $2. Per-user add-ons cost $125 per user per month and Max Edition starts at $550 per user per month. The pricing page says nothing about guardrails. — **high** (https://www.salesforce.com/agentforce/pricing/)
- **Sierra.** Charges by outcome ("gets paid only when we complete a task for you"). Unresolved and escalated conversations are generally not billed, and outcome criteria are agreed per contract. No list prices. The product page claims it connects to "systems of record" to process claims and returns, and offers Ghostwriter (configure behaviour and guardrails in natural language), built-in testing, and Explorer (natural-language analytics). — **medium** (https://sierra.ai/blog/outcome-based-pricing-for-ai-agents, https://sierra.ai/product)
- **Decagon.** "Agent Operating Procedures" (AOPs) are natural-language SOPs compiled into logic, with Git versioning, action calls shown on a timeline, and reasoning that can be traced. Watchtower runs QA on 100% of conversations against custom criteria. "Duet" proposes updates to the agent, and each change needs human approval. Tools are written in Python with MCP support. All of this is the vendor's own claim. — **medium** (https://decagon.ai/product/aop, https://decagon.ai/vs/sierra)
- **Ada.** Offers API tools, code tools that hold policy logic, and MCP tools that can be switched on or off one by one. A "Reasoning Engine", Playbooks for multi-step procedures, simulations before launch, and "Coaching" where feedback on past conversations is applied automatically to future ones. No approval step is described before actions run. Pricing is per conversation or per resolution, with no public numbers. — **medium** (https://www.ada.cx/platform/)
- **Gorgias.** AI Agent costs $0.90 per automated interaction on annual billing, $1.00 monthly, and $1.50 above the plan's allowance. Each one also uses up a helpdesk ticket. If a human agent replies within 72 hours of an AI resolution, it is billed as a normal ticket instead. Plans range from $10 to $900 a month. — **high** (https://www.gorgias.com/pricing)
- **Freshdesk Freddy.** AI Agent sessions cost $49 per 100 after a one-time free 500. An email session is a 72-hour window. Copilot is $29 per agent per month (Pro and Enterprise only). There are separate meters for MCP actions ($15 per 1,000) and connector tasks ($80 per 5,000). The pricing page does not describe what the agent can actually do. — **high** (https://www.freshworks.com/freshdesk/pricing/)
- **Forethought.** Five products: Solve (agent, reasons through policies via "Autoflows"), Triage (intent, sentiment and urgency tagging, plus routing), Assist (copilot), Discover (finds knowledge gaps) and QA (scores human tickets). It is aimed at large teams: it asks for "20,000+ historical tickets and at least 2,000 email or chat tickets per month". The FAQ describes no approval workflow. — **medium** (https://forethought.ai/faq)
- **Kustomer.** Publishes no prices ("doesn't post one-size-fits-all pricing"). It lists Concierge, Architect, Multi-Agent Mode, an MCP Server, and "AI Evaluations & Conversation Safeguards", but does not explain any of them. — **low** (https://www.kustomer.com/pricing/)

### QA and analytics tools
- **Zendesk QA (formerly Klaus).** AutoQA "evaluates 100% of AI agent conversations" against built-in categories (tone, empathy, comprehension, grammar) or custom criteria. Spotlights flag looping bots, churn risk and escalations, and a real-time QA feature is in early access. The page says root-cause findings "can be used to refine AI agent instructions" but does not explain how. — **medium** (https://www.zendesk.com/service/quality-assurance/qa-for-ai-agents/)
- **MaestroQA became Rippit on 24 Feb 2026.** The company now sells itself as a "conversation data platform", with QA as one of 13 use cases. The founder said QA is "branded … as the past in an AI-first world". Rippit's "Chatbot Monitoring" reviews every bot conversation for wrong answers and coverage gaps, then *drafts SOP updates for teams to approve*. — **medium** (https://kaizo.com/blog/maestroqa-is-now-rippit/ [competitor blog], https://www.rippit.com/)
- **Level AI and Observe.AI.** Both claim to score 100% of interactions and sell auto-QA, live agent assist, coaching and AI agents. Observe.AI says its agents read and write CRM and backend data and "track every action". No prices are published and the figures are vendor claims. — **medium** (https://thelevel.ai/, https://www.observe.ai/)

### Failure stories
- **Air Canada (Moffatt, BC Civil Resolution Tribunal, 14 Feb 2024).** The chatbot said a bereavement fare refund could be claimed within 90 days after travel. The airline's own policy page said it could not. The tribunal rejected the argument that the chatbot was "a separate legal entity" and ordered Air Canada to pay roughly $600. — **high** (https://www.computerworld.com/article/1612087/air-canada-chatbot-error-underscores-ais-enterprise-liability-danger.html)
- **Cursor "Sam" (April 2025).** The AI email-support bot told users that being logged out on several machines was "expected behavior" under a policy that did not exist. Co-founder Truell: "We have no such policy." The real cause was a race condition in session handling. Some users cancelled, and Cursor now labels AI replies. — **high** (https://www.theregister.com/2025/04/18/cursor_ai_support_bot_lies/, https://fortune.com/article/customer-support-ai-cursor-went-rogue)
- **Klarna.** Its AI handled 75% of chats (2.3M conversations) by February 2024. In May 2025 the CEO said cost had driven decisions too much, that quality fell, and that "there will always be a human if you want". Klarna began hiring human agents again. — **high** (https://tech.co/news/klarna-reverses-ai-overhaul)
- **Fin's resolution counting is disputed.** One customer found 16 of 40 tickets marked "resolved by Fin", judged 9 of those not actually resolved, and could not override the label. Intercom staff admitted a "misstep" in how billing had been explained (June 2025) and promised no fix or timeline. — **medium** (https://community.intercom.com/fin-product-feedback-member-group-49/fin-s-flawed-resolution-assumption-10516)
- **Fin hallucination regressions.** Users reported (Nov 2025 to Jan 2026) that Fin suddenly gave wrong answers or attributed features that were not in its sources, and that "guidance" did not reliably stop it. Intercom staff asked whether the customers were using Tasks or Procedures. — **medium** (https://community.intercom.com/deploy-fin-96/fin-suddenly-hallucinates-and-no-longer-responds-correctly-13286)
- **Following policy is still the weak spot.** On τ-bench, the best agent (GPT-4o) averaged under 50% success, and its pass^8 on retail was about 25%. "Function calling agents are not great at following rules provided in the policy documents." These are 2024 models, but this is the open benchmark Sierra itself publishes. — **high** for the benchmark result, **low** for how well it describes today's models (https://sierra.ai/uk/blog/benchmarking-ai-agents, https://github.com/sierra-research/tau2-bench)
- **Customers distrust AI support.** In a Gartner survey of 5,728 customers (Dec 2023), 64% would prefer companies not use AI in service, and 53% would consider switching. The top fear is that it becomes harder to reach a person. — **medium** (https://insurance-canada.ca/2024/07/15/gartner-survey-ai-for-customer-service/)

### Local angle (SKELAR, the organiser)
- SKELAR's consumer businesses work in EdTech, mental health, pet care and FashionTech, with more than 10 million users. Support runs night shifts in English, and refund demands and technical problems are escalated to other departments. — **medium** (https://jobs.dou.ua/companies/skelar/vacancies/372520/)
- A SKELAR support role covers "retaining users who request refunds" and processing refunds in payment tools, all in **Freshdesk**. The role has KPIs including a target refund rate and a career path to a "QC Agent" role. So refunds, retention and QC are the organiser's own day-to-day work. — **medium** (https://jobs.dou.ua/companies/skelar/vacancies/330574/, https://jobs.dou.ua/companies/skelar/vacancies/375717/)

## Evidence against / open doubts
- **Approvals with a human in the loop are not new.** Fin (the Loop-in-teammate step) and Zendesk (agent-approved and pre-approved actions) both ship them. Pitching "HITL approvals" on its own as innovation will not hold up. (https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/14468561, https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/9174548349978-About-actions-for-auto-assist-and-action-flows)
- **"Closing the loop from QA back into the bot" also exists in some form.** Fin Insights Suggestions, Rippit's drafted SOP updates, Decagon's Duet with human approval, Ada's Coaching, and Zendesk QA root cause feeding into instructions all do some version of it. What none of these sources show is the loop working on **action and policy compliance**. They all target content, tone or SOP text. (https://fin.ai/updates/fin-insights, https://www.rippit.com/, https://decagon.ai/vs/sierra, https://www.ada.cx/platform/)
- **Simulations and testing are standard** in Fin, Decagon, Ada and Sierra. Agentforce's Testing Center turned up only in search snippets and was not fetched.
- **Reasoning traces exist too.** Decagon says "every reasoning step is traceable". A competitor review claims customers still find auditing "inconsistent in practice", but that comes from a rival vendor (eesel) and has no quotes. (https://www.eesel.ai/blog/decagon-ai-review)
- **Much of the published criticism comes from competitors.** eesel, Kaizo, Macha and Fini sell rival products, so the weaknesses they report are weak evidence. I left out pricing figures that appeared only in third-party snippets (Decagon's roughly $95K+, Ada's per-resolution estimates, Zendesk QA at $35 per agent, Observe.AI add-on prices).
- **The τ-bench numbers date from 2024.** Newer models probably score higher. The finding that agents are unreliable over repeated runs is still the reason pass^k exists. I did not check the current leaderboard at taubench.com.
- **The good outcomes are real.** Klarna's 2.3M chats and Fin's "76%" example deployments show these products deliver value. The gap is trust and control, not whether AI can act.

## What this means for the idea
- **Don't pitch "an AI agent that takes actions" or "human approval" as the novelty.** Fin and Zendesk already ship both. Pitch the **independent audit layer** the incumbents structurally can't offer, because they bill on the resolutions they count themselves (see the Fin resolution-count dispute and outcome pricing at Fin, Zendesk, Sierra and Gorgias). That means:
  - each proposed action cites the exact policy clause that permits it,
  - risky actions (refund above X, account deletion) wait at a visible approval gate,
  - an evaluator scores every finished conversation on **"was the action correct under policy?"**, not on tone,
  - repeated failures turn into a suggested policy or guideline edit that a human approves.

  This covers all four challenge requirements: intent, action, decision support and evaluation.
- **Build the demo on the organiser's own pain.** Use a simulated B2C subscription app like SKELAR's portfolio: refund or cancel requests, retention offers, a target refund rate, Freshdesk-style tickets, and a "QC Agent" persona. Use the Air Canada and Cursor cases in the pitch. Show the bot *refusing* to invent a policy: no clause found, so it escalates instead of answering.
- **Borrow rather than invent.** Take the policy, tools and tasks format from τ²-bench (MIT). Run a small pass^k reliability check on our Gemma agent and show the number. That gives a technical-execution story none of the closed vendors publishes. Copy Parlant's idea of condition-action guidelines with an explanation trace, but implement it ourselves in `agent/` with Pydantic-validated Gemma output.
- **Open source is a real differentiator.** Every commercial product here is closed and sales-led, or tied to its own suite. The open helpdesks (Chatwoot, Zammad, FreeScout) don't have this auditable acting and QA layer. An Apache or MIT repo also targets the "Best Open-Source AI Project" prize.
- **Keep the demo scope honest.** Use one product, 3–4 actions (look up the subscription, issue a refund, apply a retention discount, escalate), one policy document, one approval gate, and one QA dashboard that shows a failure being caught and the guideline fix being approved. Mocked payment APIs are fine if they are clearly simulated and the AI reasoning on top is real.
