# Users and pain: who has the support problem, and how badly?

**Answer in one line:** The pain is sharpest exactly where SKELAR lives: consumer subscription apps sold on the web (Liven, PawChamp, RiseGuide, DramaShorts, Brighterly…). Their customers' angriest reviews are mostly about **surprise renewals, cancellations that "didn't work", and refused refunds**. Each of those tickets needs a lookup and a judgement call before anyone can act. Meanwhile the agents (shift-based, English-speaking Ukrainian teams) spend more than half of their time away from customers: gathering context, doing the actual task and writing it up. QA reviews only a few percent of chats.

Research date: 2026-10-31 (the day of the hackathon). Trustpilot samples are the 20 most recent 1-star reviews per brand on that date. The fetch tool categorised them and I did not hand-check each one, so treat the percentages as approximate.

## Key findings

### SKELAR: who the judges are and what they run
- **SKELAR is now a fund/venture builder for consumer tech, and its portfolio is almost entirely B2C subscription apps for English-speaking global markets.** Its homepage lists Liven (well-being / self-discovery), PawChamp (dog training), DramaShorts (short-drama video), StorybyWriter, AlphaNovel (novels), Dressly (AI stylist), Spirio (spiritual well-being), Brighterly (kids' maths and reading tutoring), unnamed job tools ("Boost your chances of getting hired", "AI-powered job search", "Fast one-click editing"), RiseGuide (expert insights), Leaply, MaxBeauty, HabitONE (nootropic coffee, physical goods) and Relatio (couples). It claims "10M+ people" use products built by its businesses. The same page lists "PolyTech Code Fest x SKELAR, Lviv, October 31" among its events — **high** (https://www.skelar.tech/, https://jobs.dou.ua/companies/skelar/vacancies/376810/)
- Note: **skelar.com is a different, unrelated product** (a resource-management tool for agencies). The venture builder is skelar.tech — **high** (https://www.skelar.com/)
- **Support in SKELAR businesses is in-house, multi-channel and runs 24/7 in English.** Ukrainian agents work shifts that include about 6 night shifts a month (23:00–08:00), handle email, chat and phone, and escalate complex cases to other teams. Zendesk or Freshdesk is the named helpdesk (a "nice to have"). Postings: PawChamp, Liven, Dressly. PawChamp agents also give dog-training advice by chat. Dressly is building its own in-house support team — **high** (https://jobs.dou.ua/companies/skelar/vacancies/311379/, https://jobs.dou.ua/companies/skelar/vacancies/310214/, https://jobs.dou.ua/companies/skelar/vacancies/330742/, https://jobs.dou.ua/companies/skelar/vacancies/375717/)
- **SKELAR support teams are big enough to have leads, QC specialists and separate billing/retention teams.** Brighterly's support team has 36 agents in 2 teams, each with its own lead. The lead sends weekly performance reports, tracks shift performance and "works closely with QC specialists" to pass feedback to agents. Billing and retention are separate teams that support works with, which means hand-offs. Spirio mentions Shift Lead and QC career paths — **medium** (https://jobs.dou.ua/companies/skelar/vacancies/335044/)

### End customers: what they hate (SKELAR portfolio, their own words)
- **On every SKELAR brand checked, "unexpected charge / renewal" is the top 1-star complaint, followed by cancellation that didn't stick and refused refunds.** Recent 1-star sample (20 reviews each):
  - **Liven** (TrustScore 4.3, 35,575 reviews, 13% 1-star): unexpected charge/renewal in 18/20, cancellation 8/20, refund refused 6/20, chatbot or no reply 6/20. Quotes: "Cancelled my subscription on 25th Sept 26, still was recently charged again."; "I received automated responses from a bot."; "I have been waiting for a week for a response regarding my refund".
  - **PawChamp** (4.4, 21,037 reviews, 11% 1-star): unexpected charge 13/20, refund refused 7/20, cancellation 5/20, account access 4/20. Quotes: "I also cannot login so cannot cancel"; "actually cancelling membership requires accessing multiple levels of hidden menus"; "When you unexpectedly get charged, they tell you it's too late for a refund".
  - **RiseGuide** (4.4, 28,396 reviews, 4% 1-star): unexpected charge 14/20, cancellation 6/20, account access 4/20. Quotes: "My auto-renewal was OFF, yet RiseGuide still charged my credit card."; "If you try and find your account using a password reset none of your emails are recognised"; "Their generic replies and lack of resolution have made this experience extremely frustrating."
  - **DramaShorts** (4.4, 579 reviews, 16% 1-star): unexpected or duplicate charge 14/20. Quotes: "I get a subscription and I get charged 5 times in the same month"; "I have asked for a refund but have only received an automated response."; "Cannot cancel the subscription. Sent me to a third party pay site for $38.99".
  - Confidence **high** that this is the dominant pattern; **low–medium** on the exact counts (small, machine-tagged samples). (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/theliven.com?stars=1, https://www.trustpilot.com/review/theliven.com, https://www.trustpilot.com/review/paw-champ.com?stars=1, https://www.trustpilot.com/review/riseguide.com?stars=1, https://www.trustpilot.com/review/dramashorts.io?stars=1)
- **Companies answer these reviews with templates, not by resolving them.** PawChamp replied to all 20 1-star reviews and every reply moved the customer to email (reviews-trustpilot@…). About 11 of 20 replies argue that the terms "were shown at checkout", and none publicly confirms a full refund. Liven's reply says "subscriptions automatically renew unless canceled before the next billing date". Both profiles say they reply to 99% of negative reviews within 24h. So the tickets do exist, they arrive through extra channels, and an agent has to look each one up by hand — **medium** (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/paw-champ.com?stars=1, https://www.trustpilot.com/review/theliven.com)
- **The tickets behind these reviews are action tickets, not FAQ questions.** Each one needs the agent to: find the account (often by a different email; customers say "I NEVER MADE AN ACCT", "cannot login"), check the billing history in the payment provider, check whether and when cancellation happened, apply the refund policy (e.g. Brighterly: first 10 days; BetterMe reportedly asked a user to "earn my refund by showing … 7 workouts"), then refund or cancel and reply — **medium** (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/riseguide.com?stars=1, https://www.trustpilot.com/review/betterme.world?stars=1)
- **This is industry-wide in subscription apps, not just a SKELAR problem.** BetterMe, a Ukrainian-founded peer with 115,962 reviews, shows the same mix (unexpected charge in 9/20 1-star reviews, refund refused in 5/20). An Appbot analysis of 11,448 reviews of one subscription health app found 6,510 were payment-related. Those payment reviews were 67% 1-star versus 23% overall and averaged 1.8 stars versus 3.7. Quotes: "I cancelled before the trial ended but was still charged.", "I was charged twice." — **high** (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/betterme.world?stars=1, https://appbot.co/blog/subscription-cancellation-issues-app-ratings/)
- **Regulators treat it as a growing harm.** The US FTC received "nearly 70 consumer complaints per day on average" about negative-option/subscription practices in 2024, up from 42 per day in 2021. Its Click-to-Cancel rule requires "a simple mechanism to cancel … and immediately halt charges". I did not verify the rule's current legal status: there was litigation, and I recall but could not confirm that it was vacated in 2025 — **high** for the complaint numbers (https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/10/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-click-cancel-rule-making-it-easier-consumers-end-recurring)
- **Customers hate bots that talk but cannot act.** The CFPB's report on bank chatbots quotes complainants: "I am being sent in an endless loop with no way out." It also describes a bot that promised a dispute and credit within 48h, after which "the dispute is nowhere to be found". It cites a survey in which 80% of consumers felt more frustrated after a chatbot and 78% needed a human afterwards — **medium** (https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/chatbots-in-consumer-finance/chatbots-in-consumer-finance/)

### Support agents and leads: what eats their time
- **Agents spend less than half their time with customers.** Salesforce State of Service, 7th edition (6,500 service professionals, 40 countries, 2025): reps spend 46% of their time with customers, "due in part to administrative tasks and internal responsibilities"; manually entering case notes is one of the named time sinks. 82% say customer expectations are higher than before — **medium** (one survey, vendor-run but large) (https://www.salesforce.com/en-us/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/documents/PDF/state-of-service-7th-edition.pdf)
- **Routine cases are about half the caseload.** In the same report, routine cases are 48% of reps' caseload at organisations without AI agents and 43% with them. Respondents expect AI to resolve 50% of cases by 2027, up from 30% in 2025. Leaders using AI agents expect costs and resolution time to fall by about 20% — **medium** (https://www.salesforce.com/en-us/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/documents/PDF/state-of-service-7th-edition.pdf)
- **Per interaction, the overhead is measured in minutes.** Verint State of Agent Experience 2026 (1,000 agents in contact centres of 300+ seats, surveyed Nov–Dec 2025, ±3.1%): average time per call is 3.2 min gathering context, 2.7 min searching knowledge, 3.8 min completing the task and 3.0 min on after-call work. 57% of calls need context-gathering and 45% need a knowledge search; task completion and after-call work appear in 54% and 67% of calls (the PDF layout makes it unclear which figure goes with which). Agents' own words: "too much time completing routine tasks – like issuing a refund, resetting a password…". 31% of agents say they are likely to leave within 6 months. Verint's CMO puts the cost of replacing an agent at about $20,000 — **medium** (vendor survey, contact-centre and voice-heavy) (https://www.verint.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/the-state-of-agent-experience-2026-research-report.pdf)
- **AI assistance helps novices most, so it suits shift-based junior teams.** In Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond's study (NBER w31161, 5,179 support agents), an AI assistant raised issues resolved per hour by 14% on average and by 34% for novice and low-skilled agents, with "minimal impact on experienced" agents. It also improved customer sentiment and retention. This is the strongest independent evidence in the field (peer-reviewed in QJE 2025) — **high** (https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161)
- **Manual QA covers only a sliver of conversations.** The commonly cited benchmark is about 2% ("the common industry benchmark for manual QA coverage"; "most firms sample fewer than 5%"). Aggregated vendor figures: 2–5% (AmplifAI/Solidroad), 1–2% for legacy QA (Intryc), and Blueground at 3% coverage before AI QA and 5.5% after. Every source is a vendor of automated QA and I found no independent study — **medium** for "single-digit percent", **low** for any exact number (https://aveni.ai/blog/182408-calls-nobody-reviewed/, https://stealthagents.com/research/customer-support-qa-statistics-2026, https://www.zendesk.hk/blog/auto-qa/)
- **Macro libraries become a burden of their own.** Agents must read the ticket, infer the goal, then pick from hundreds of macros that "can't see information from other systems" — **low** (vendor blog) (https://eesel.ai/blog/macro-actions)
- **Ukrainian support outsourcers are being asked for exactly this.** SupportYourApp (1,500+ people, 60 languages, clients including Calm and MacPaw) hires consultants to recommend "NLP-driven ticket classification, auto-triage, and agent assist" and to improve CSAT, FCR and AHT across Zendesk, Freshdesk and Intercom — **medium** (https://jobs.dou.ua/companies/supportyourapp/vacancies/366990/)

## Evidence against / open doubts
- **Customers distrust AI in support.** In Gartner's survey (5,728 customers, Dec 2023), 64% would prefer companies didn't use AI for service and 53% would consider switching. The top fear is not being able to reach a human. This argues for an **agent-side copilot with a human approving actions**, not a customer-facing autonomous bot (https://insurance-canada.ca/2024/07/15/gartner-survey-ai-for-customer-service/)
- **Klarna went "too far" with AI.** It had claimed its AI did the work of 700 agents and handled 75% of chats. Its CEO later said automation "went too far" and that cost was "a too predominant evaluation factor", and Klarna began rehiring humans in May 2025 (https://www.maginative.com/article/klarna-dials-back-its-ai-customer-service-strategy-now-its-hiring-humans-again/)
- **Part of the pain is business-model design, not support tooling.** Renewals without reminders, hidden cancellation and strict refund policies are product and policy choices. Replies like "terms were shown at checkout" show the company defending its revenue. An AI that simply grants every refund works against the judges' businesses. The right framing is **consistent, policy-grounded decisions plus fewer escalations and chargebacks**, and it should surface patterns such as "renewal-without-reminder drives 40% of refund tickets" to product (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/paw-champ.com?stars=1)
- **App-store billing limits what support can do.** For iOS in-app purchases only Apple can refund, so support can only explain and redirect. Appbot notes that users blame the app when they can't find where to cancel. SKELAR apps appear to sell largely through web checkout (reviews mention bank and card charges and third-party pay pages), which keeps refunds in the company's hands, but I have not verified this for each product (https://appbot.co/blog/subscription-cancellation-issues-app-ratings/, https://www.trustpilot.com/review/dramashorts.io?stars=1)
- **The best time-use numbers come from voice contact centres** with 300+ seats (Verint) or broad surveys (Salesforce). SKELAR teams work in chat and email at 30–40 agents. After-call work is different in chat, though context-gathering and task execution still apply.
- **Positive reviews dominate.** These brands score 4.3–4.4 overall with 4–16% 1-star reviews, so support isn't broken at SKELAR. The opportunity is efficiency and consistency at scale, not rescue (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/theliven.com, https://www.trustpilot.com/review/riseguide.com?stars=1)
- **Ukrainian-consumer angle is weak for this audience.** SKELAR products serve English-speaking global users. Ukrainian sources mostly show Ukrainian *agents* doing the work. Local consumer examples, such as monobank review complaints and NovaPay moving support in-app because of phishing through fake support chats, are relevant only as a security side note (https://interfax.com.ua/news/telecom/1123785.html)

## What this means for the idea
- **Demo scenario that will land with SKELAR judges:** a simulated web-billed wellness or pet-training subscription app (a Liven- or PawChamp-like product). The ticket: "I cancelled but you charged me £39.99 again, refund now". The AI identifies the real goal (stop future charges, get money back, maybe keep access), finds the account even under a different email, reads the billing and cancellation history, checks the refund policy (e.g. a window in days, previous refunds, usage), and **proposes a concrete action**: cancel now, refund the last charge (full or partial), or offer a pause. It shows its reasoning and cites the policy, the agent approves with one click, the tool executes it and drafts the reply. This is exactly the top 1-star pattern on Liven, PawChamp, RiseGuide and DramaShorts.
- **Keep a human in the loop for money actions.** Gartner's 64%, the Klarna rollback and the CFPB "doom loop" evidence all say customer-facing autonomy is risky. Pitch it as a copilot for the 36-agent night shift. Brynjolfsson's +34% for novices fits teams whose leads "grew from juniors".
- **Close the loop with analytics:** score 100% of the conversations a lead currently can't review (manual QA is about 2%), and cluster refund reasons (e.g. "renewed without a reminder", "can't log in to cancel", "duplicate charge") into a weekly insight for product and billing. This covers challenge points 3 and 4, and fits leads who already send weekly reports and work with QC specialists.
- **The ROI slide writes itself from cited numbers:** about 3.2 min of context-gathering, 3.8 min of task execution and 3.0 min of after-call work per contact (Verint); reps spend 46% of their time with customers (Salesforce); 64–90% of recent 1-star reviews are billing complaints. Present these as "industry data", not as SKELAR's own numbers.
- **Don't build "AI grants every refund".** Frame it as policy-consistent decisions that reduce chargebacks, public 1-star reviews and agent time. That respects the judges' unit economics.
